# Books to Life and the Reading Pod
## Iteration history through 17 November 2026

The project began as a local speech-to-text client. It grew into a system that follows a reader through a book, decides when there is enough evidence to play an effect, and projects that effect across three calibrated walls. The biggest change was separating those jobs. Recognizing a word, advancing the reading position, firing a checkpoint, and getting a scene onto the wall each have their own failure modes and their own tests.

The current Windows pod path is the React operator panel, a Windows microphone client, Parakeet NeMo in WSL, the shared Python matcher, and MapMap for projection. **KeenASR was implemented and tested in a separate browser prototype.** It performed better on a small paired Tea and Mermaid replay. The larger Keen callback replays also showed gains from matcher changes. The Windows panel and the Keen prototype were developed on different branches, so those results do not describe a Keen option in the current panel.

### The isolated laptop sweep

Before standardizing the WSL setup, the isolated harness compared Whisper English Base/Small/Medium with and without hints, **native Parakeet Unified F16 on Vulkan** at three audio buffer settings, and Moonshine Medium through native Vulkan and the official quantized CPU runtime. It included a 500 ms minimum decode interval trial for native Moonshine and a single-thread CPU trial for the official runtime. This F16 Parakeet benchmark is separate from the later Q8 CPU comparison.

The saved two-recording aggregate shows why we kept cue counts, skips and WER in separate columns. On Tea, F16 Parakeet's 1.12 s buffer had about 46.5% WER against the supplied passage and fired 1/3 cues. Hinted Whisper Small had about 74.4% WER and fired 3/3. Native Moonshine Vulkan fired 1/3. On Fat Cat, F16 Parakeet at 1.12 s fired 2/2 configured cues, with one on an anchor the reader had skipped. Native Moonshine Vulkan fired only that skipped anchor. The official CPU Moonshine trials fired 1/3 on Tea and 0/2 on Fat Cat across the tested variants. These are one real-paced replay per profile on two recordings with intended-passage references. They show tradeoffs and a cue-policy failure, with limited model-ranking value. [Harness and profile descriptions](../benchmarks/README.md)

### What we learned about hints

We tried **off**, **static or whole-passage context**, and **moving book-region hints**. The hints change what the recognizer is likely to output. The Python matcher still has to check that the observed words belong to the passage and satisfy the cue. A hint can help an unusual title word or name. It can also push a different line toward a cue or change when a word becomes stable.

In one 27-cue hint comparison, hints off fired 26 with a 0.830 s median delay, moving hints fired 26 with a 0.797 s median and one Billy Goats cue 6.083 s late, and whole-passage hints fired 25 with a 0.874 s median and one Tom cue almost 20 s early. A later no-hints check fired 26/27 with a 0.886 s median and none over a second early. Those batches support using hints off for the first pod run and evaluating the effect of hints per book. [Hint experiment](../benchmarks/READING_CONTEXT_RESULTS.md) · [Verified setup comparison](../benchmarks/BEST_SETUP_RESULTS.md)

## 3. Reading tracking and cue permission

The story data can contain the full authored passage, individual checkpoints, required words, and effect assignments. The reading tracker estimates position from changing ASR snapshots. The effect gate checks recent support for the cue, enforces the policy and records the outcome. The operator panel displays the same matching evidence used by the matcher. Sound and projection are separate effects of an accepted checkpoint.

| Tracking style | What we tried and why |
| --- | --- |
| Fuzzy phrase tracking, `fuzzy` | Compared a short rolling transcript with authored checkpoint phrases using similarity, coverage and stability. This was the first useful demo. A phrase-only tracker had little information about where a reader had jumped or which repeated phrase belonged to the current page. |
| Word alignment, `alignment` | Aligned observed words against the authored passage and maintained a position. Insertions, omissions and substitutions could be represented explicitly. Bounded forward search and look-back supported skips and rereads. Correct sentence position could still arrive before the spoken word that should release an effect. |
| Evidence across updates | Retained useful support from partial snapshots when the recognizer revised its text. Confirmation distinguished independent evidence from repeats of the same snapshot. This reduced dependence on a single clean final transcript. |
| Split words, compound words and chunk boundaries | Tested split and boundary matchers. An observed adjacent pair could support one authored word under bounded rules. The original ASR text stayed available for the display and audit. Preserving acquired phrase evidence across a chunk boundary helped the actual cue end survive a transcript revision. |
| Timely evidence, selected as `split` in the pod CLI | Combines reading position with a nearby cue’s recent evidence and endpoint. Uses stable support and bounded context, with tighter requirements for one-shots. The CLI name survives the earlier experiments. The current implementation includes contextual treatment of articles and constrained pronunciation comparisons. |
| Authored effect cue and interrupted reading, later research branch | Added optional `effect_cue` text inside a longer tracking checkpoint. Tested keeping a supported prefix through a short pause or off-book aside, allowing optional lead-in words where context supports it, tightening short forward jumps, and retaining a near-complete effect for a bounded recovery window. These experiments are branch-specific. |

A missing **a/an/the** can be forgiven in context. Missing content words can reduce coverage or block an effect, especially if the story requires them. A sound-alike receives credit only under the applicable context, support and ambiguity rules. The colors in the panel explain what happened to the observed text. They are an explanation of the match, and their presence alone grants no additional cue permission. Early spelling/phonetic comparisons admitted realistic wrong words such as *coat/goat* and *seal/zeal* in controls. Later feature tests exposed *fan/van*, *pen/pin* and *sink/zinc*. A stricter endpoint experiment rejected these false matches, then lost seven cues in a saved Keen replay; it was not kept as a blanket rule. [Runtime matcher](../books_to_life/matching/timely_matcher.py) · [Effect policy](EFFECT_POLICIES.md) · [Later endpoint review](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/3e47745/docs/POD_MATCHER_FOLLOWUP.md)

The one-shot rule prevents an already-fired cue from replaying when a recognizer revises the transcript or the reader repeats it. If the reader skips a page, the matcher can acquire a later supported cue. An earlier skipped one-shot stays skipped unless a tested policy explicitly allows recovery; ambience can be authored to catch up in supported circumstances. On the rendering side a jump to a valid cue can also skip unstaged scenes. The two decisions are independent.

### Specific failures and fixes

- **Finishing during decoder lookahead.** An accepted cue could appear in ASR only after audio ended. We first tested extra tail time as a diagnostic, then implemented bounded EOF completion in the decoder. The decoder can use digital silence for remaining right context without extending the captured utterance or fabricating spoken words. The regression added two cue emissions across 51 recorded readings and kept four omission controls quiet. [Benchmark index](../benchmarks/README.md)
- **Pause, adult interruption and delayed continuation.** We tested preserving a uniquely supported prefix across a pause or completed off-book aside, then requiring observed continuation. The later Parakeet follow-up held an acquired near-complete cue for up to 30 seconds. Fresh 1× replays regained the Mermaid effect after 10-, 15-, and 20-second Singlish asides, while all three stray-endpoint controls stayed off. Both recognizers still missed Mermaid’s second effect in the paired case when the adult interrupted right before its required endpoint. Tests cover cases where a parent can finish a phrase; they do not establish which physical speaker said each word. [November research summary](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/90a8710/benchmarks/README.md) · [Paired handoff](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/ae41579/docs/POD_PC_HANDOFF.md)
- **A valid internal sound-alike.** A human accepted the child’s sheep phrase while ASR supplied a near neighbor. Bounded acquisition in the later research branch recovered that case using ordered context and an exact endpoint. A different case with ASR substituting the required word *lost* remained blocked. The subsequent authored policy made *lost* essential and kept the miss visible. [Effect cue regression](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/90a8710/benchmarks/README.md)
- **Distant short matches and prompted words.** Forward-jump experiments recovered valid later cues while restricting short phrases far ahead of the known position. The first quoted-prompt guard blocked ten scripted prompts but also blocked valid positive coaching, so the follow-up restored the shared-reading rule. Main also tested an isolated finalized “T” treatment. The “T” experiment was reverted. These fixes belong in the shared matcher and authored policies after paired regression, rather than introducing general transcript replacements. [Forward-jump follow-up](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/3e47745/docs/POD_MATCHER_FOLLOWUP.md) · [Revert](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/commit/63e2a00)


## 4. KeenASR in detail

### From browser demo to shared matcher

1. **Browser recognition and local alignment.** The October prototype ran KeenASR on-device in the browser, showed the transcript and tracked the current word. The first JavaScript tracker accepted exact words, sufficiently long shared stems and near spellings; it also gave some alignment substitutions partial credit. A tolerant ordered scan of the transcript tail let the reader recover a phrase by rereading. This was an early broad tolerance design, separate from the later shared effect gate. [Keen prototype history](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/commits/keenasr-first/)
2. **Live microphone and repeatable recorded audio.** The same UI could select the microphone or feed a recording to the SDK through a browser audio stream. The recording played at real time, so a callback could be timed against a known clip. The local prototype has a 25-second listening window, a two-minute attempt ceiling, reliable Stop, and end-of-attempt reading accuracy and word error rate. These scores are distinct from cue accuracy. [Prototype setup](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/21750e9/README.md)
3. **Shared guarded cues.** Commit `551fd91` brought the Python matcher into the Keen prototype at stability 2. The browser sends text callbacks to a localhost Python server; audio stays in the browser. Tea and Mermaid manifests gave both engines cue IDs and shared policy. This made it possible to compare cue precision, missed cues and callback timing without comparing a permissive browser matcher to a stricter NeMo matcher. The prototype logged cues; live projector control remained a separate project path. [Guarded Keen integration](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/commit/551fd91)
4. **Tea and parent handoff.** Commit `21750e9` tested a Tea-specific oral-reading decoding graph with *tea pot* and scoped the equivalent *teapot* handling to the Tea stove cue’s matcher input. The raw browser transcript remained visible. Cumulative callback handling allowed a supported handoff or final phrase to complete once. This was a narrow decoder/cue trial. It should not become a global word substitution. [Tea decoder and handoff change](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/commit/21750e9)
5. **More recordings and forward-jump recovery.** We replayed saved callback histories and tested a short distant cue guard. The heavier endpoint experiment dropped the broader Keen replay from 169 to 163 emissions, recovering one and losing seven. A separate forward-jump change replayed the earlier 169-cue baseline and reached 175/184. Five of its added emissions came from one gardening session. This establishes what the matcher did with those callbacks, with no fresh Keen recognition or timing measurement in that comparison. [Replay follow-up](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/3e47745/docs/POD_MATCHER_FOLLOWUP.md) · [Keen forward-jump change](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/commit/997f4b1)

The web prototype requires the separately provisioned Keen SDK, its JavaScript/WASM components and the matching licensed acoustic model and graph. It uses a localhost server on port 8000 with cross-origin isolation headers and a matching gzip model response. The prototype imports Preact from a CDN on first load, which is a dependency for an offline handoff to resolve. The web license and files need to be confirmed for the target PC; Linux SDK access and ARM64 performance remain unverified. Keep the SDK assets and raw evaluation material out of any general handoff unless covered by the relevant permissions. [Prototype requirements](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/21750e9/README.md) · [KeenASR Web documentation](https://keenresearch.com/keenasr-docs/keenasr-web-introduction.html) · [Keen acoustic models and decoding graphs](https://depot.keenresearch.com/keenasr-docs/keenasr-decoding-graphs-acoustic-models.html)

## 5. Evaluation and failure diagnosis

The test setup evolved from listening to a demo into a repeatable evaluation library. We preserved the source recording alongside its story text and checkpoint manifest. For selected passages we added word-level annotations and used those to estimate when a cue should fire. Later tests fixed audio inputs and their order before a run, recorded model and runtime hashes, and compared the application’s events with a replay observer on the same callbacks.

These four measurements answer different questions.

| Method | What it isolates |
| --- | --- |
| Inspect the transcript and align known words | Where the recognizer substituted, omitted or mis-split a word. A human listening pass is needed to distinguish a real reader omission from a recognition error. |
| Replay fixed ASR callbacks through old and new matchers | What a matcher change added, lost, delayed, duplicated or falsely triggered on identical input. |
| Stream a WAV once at 1× over the real client/server path | Recognition and effect timing under packet pacing. It checks audio delivery, interim results, end-of-stream drain and matcher observer agreement. |
| Measure the live pod | Capture and room acoustics, projector performance, playback timing and what the reader actually sees and hears. These require the physical setup. |

### Selected results

- **Development recordings and boundary work.** The live development comparison completed 24 streams. Whisper Medium stayed at 9/16 cues, while NeMo improved from 12/16 to 16/16 with the split matcher. On selected harder word-boundary cases, an OCSC general boundary comparison rose from 2/4 to 4/4. Later work improved timing in a handful of cases without changing the tested cue total. These results justified deeper work on evidence and timing. [Live tracker comparison](../benchmarks/LIVE_TRACKER_RESULTS.md) · [Boundary comparison](../benchmarks/BOUNDARY_CONFIRMATION_RESULTS.md) · [OCSC evaluation](../benchmarks/OCSC_EVALUATION.md)
- **MPS child-reading probes and held-out speakers.** The post-phonetic Nov 2 frozen batch used 24 streams from 12 previously excluded speakers and six familiar passages. It emitted 64/72 configured effects, including 46/46 provider-marked correct anchors. The later Nov 6 research branch ran a different 24-stream, 12-speaker batch. It emitted 53/72, with 14 suppressed, one skipped and four unreached; all 37 provider-marked correct anchors emitted. The batches used different speaker selections and policies. Review outcomes alongside labels before calling a withheld cue a miss. [Nov 2 pilot and held-out runs](../benchmarks/GUARDED_PILOT_RESULTS.md) · [Nov 6 research results](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/90a8710/benchmarks/README.md)
- **Singapore CHILDES diagnostic.** One classroom conversation plus three existing context clips gave six opportunities across three unique transcript-derived anchors. The initial frozen run fired zero. Later listening confirmed two false negatives, and a subsequent bounded prefix fix recovered the reviewed left-hand example. This was a diagnostic on one conversational source with background speech and known selected phrases. A representative Singapore child read-aloud pilot still needs independent children, book text and room audio. [Research diagnostic](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/90a8710/benchmarks/README.md)
- **Authored effect cues.** The later research regression left 383 saved legacy ledgers unchanged. Among 54 saved comparisons of the same authored policy, it gained 13 emissions and lost zero. It also ran 42 fresh streams. All seven declared-essential-word omissions stayed off, as did 24 off-book policy replays. The reviewed case of ASR misrecognizing an essential action word remained visible as a miss. [Authored-effect regression](https://github.com/Kinosaurus/Speech-to-Text/blob/90a8710/benchmarks/results/effect-cue-regression-2026-11-06.json)
- **Negative controls and limits.** Across 45 synthetic adult challenge inputs, five near-word tests false-triggered under literal-word grading. The other 30 negative cases stayed off and all ten correct/reread cases emitted once. Other controls covered irrelevant conversation, prompted or negated words, essential word omission and duplicate cue prevention. Synthetic and replay controls improve regression coverage; their rates do not estimate performance for a child in the room. [Guarded-pilot results](../benchmarks/GUARDED_PILOT_RESULTS.md)

The working performance measures are **fired once, withheld or suppressed, skipped, unreached, pending, unexpected, duplicate, early and late**. Each matters for a different reason. Cue coverage should be reported alongside valid-read labels and early/late event timing. WER can explain a recognition problem even when the cue still fires. Perfect cue counts can also mask late effects that break the experience.


A smaller local NLB smoke run also completed Tea, Little Mermaid and a Dog recording, with application/observer agreement on all three. Tea fired one of three configured cues, Mermaid one of two, and Dog had no configured cues. This was a transport and integration check. It did not establish how many configured phrases were in each clip or spoken as authored. The aggregate is in the local `output/nlb-ne-mo-smoke-20261108-164046/summary.json`.

## 6. Windows panel, MapMap and the three-wall pod

### From manual playback to operator controls

- **Establish the stack.** Preserved the existing checkout and used the separate first-pipeline checkout. Installed the Windows Python client from `requirements/requirements-client.txt` and built React with `npm.cmd --prefix operator-panel ci` and `npm.cmd --prefix operator-panel run build`. Started the installed Parakeet NeMo environment in WSL and verified that a Windows client got `SERVER_READY` from `127.0.0.1:9097`. Used the extracted MapMap handoff and its cue bridge. [Running instructions](RUNNING.md) · [Panel README](../operator-panel/README.md)
- **Preflight and cue inspection.** Added preflight for NeMo and the MapMap OSC socket, manual Silver Seed testing, Reset to grid, and later buttons for more scenes. The panel lists models and microphones, hints and recording toggles, checkpoint status and current matching evidence. Study sessions can show exact support, sound-alikes, missing words and forgiven articles without switching away from the live transcript. Transcripts and optional WAVs stay local. A live OSC receiver and an actual delivered picture are separate checks.
- **Story-specific cue assignment.** The panel shows which checkpoints have a MapMap projection, a local sound, both or neither, and lets the operator assign available scenes. The project started from one grid and Silver Seed. We added a second independent Sky Garden still to `Sky Garden - Two Scenes.mmp`. Both scenes use the same MapMap window. A checkpoint with only a local sound leaves projection alone.
- **Preserve the scene across cues.** The current projection stays up between checkpoints. The next projection cue changes it when ready. The scene transition is an operator setting, initially a 450 ms crossfade with other speeds and a cut available. An explicit reset, ending the experience, or loading a different story can return to the calibration grid. A new cue inside the same story can transition directly between its media scenes.
- **Fit and calibrate.** Used Windows Extend and the MapMap output on the second display. The Hitachi hookup exposed a difference between the display’s requested output geometry, the projector’s physical placement and the saved mesh. ### OSC, freeze investigation and the renderer changes

**Initial scene switching failure.** MapMap could show its fullscreen grid while the panel’s scene buttons did nothing. A launch argument such as `-p 12345` and a successful UDP send do not prove that MapMap bound the port or applied the cue. The repair covered receiver rebinding, removal of duplicate/zero-interval polling in favor of one 10 ms timer, and restoration of Windows renderer behavior needed by the handoff. The PC used port 12346 for the corrected path during these tests. We verified the chain with visible frame changes from grid to still and back in a silent display test, and the user subsequently confirmed that the Sky Garden still switched on the projector.

**Lag investigation.** The user reported that Windows felt choppy with the projector connected, and that unplugging it relieved the lag. An earlier sample recorded 35.6% total CPU with MapMap open and 17.6% with it closed. Conditions were incomplete for a controlled connected comparison. A second problem was visible in the application design: opening a project initialized and could play every video in the background even when only one was visible. The original loaded library had nine videos, one still and the calibration source. A busy OSC loop and extra video decoding could both waste resources. The remaining controlled test needs a fixed display mode and the same workload. [Lag follow-up](READING_POD_TODO.md)

**First reduction, one story in the same MapMap window.** The panel now prepares a managed project containing just the selected book’s assigned projection cues and shared calibration grid. The full shared library and the layout remain in the master. The application loads the managed project in the existing window and confirms it applied. A tracking-only book gets the grid and no story media. A quiet replay verified switching between such a book and Sky Garden in the same process. One offscreen comparison of the full project versus the Sky Garden runtime reported MapMap process CPU of 8.59% versus 4.50%, and working set of 376 versus 174 MiB. The measured story load was about 1.88 s in that test. It did not measure connected desktop smoothness. [Managed story runtime](../books_to_life/runtime/mapmap_story.py)

**Second reduction, prepare videos on demand.** Story loading brought the cue definitions into the window. It still risked opening all their video decoders. The deferred renderer now prepares the likely next video, muted and paused once its first frame is available, while the current scene remains up. A jump can request a different video directly. The bridge waits for that incoming video’s ready acknowledgement, crossfades, and releases the outgoing player. A failed or superseded preparation leaves a valid visible scene. This lets the reader jump to a well-supported later checkpoint without opening every video in between. Quiet offscreen tests saw about 0–15 ms for prepared media and 50–110 ms for cold clips before fading on this PC. Those intervals do not include speech recognition, the configured fade, projector scanout or audio onset. The updated renderer was installed after the user closed MapMap. Real-room timing and long-session resource use are open. [Scene readiness and transition code](../books_to_life/runtime/mapmap_cues.py)
